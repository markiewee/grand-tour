# Poster rules

These rules come from the feedback that shaped the first guide.

1. **Every poster carries one idea.** Tell it through contrast, scale, symbol or direction, the way 1930s travel posters did. A poster that only shows the place fails. Examples that worked:
   - A famous noodle-shop table sealed under a museum glass dome while the next table keeps eating: the relic against the living meal.
   - A bridge whose reflection becomes the sword a legend says was returned to the lake.
   - An empty milk can tipped over while egg-coffee foam rises like a sunrise: shortage turned into invention.
2. **No words on the art.** No headlines, signs, labels or logos. Put captions in the page layout instead, as one short line.
3. **A real photo behind every real place.** Attach it as a reference so buildings come out right. Blur or crop real people's faces and any signs in the reference first.
4. **One reference photo per poster where possible.** Combine two images side by side into one file when you need both.
5. **Local place names, traveller's language for everything else.**
6. **Period ephemera sits next to the poster on the page, never inside it.**
7. **Make two takes, pick one, and check it for stray lettering** with `grandtour text-check`, then by eye at full size.
8. **The traveller can ban a kind of poster.** Record the ban in the trip notes and respect it for the rest of the guide.
